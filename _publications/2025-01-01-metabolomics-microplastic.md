summary: >
  This study investigated the metabolic and biochemical responses
  of Pacific oysters (Crassostrea gigas) exposed to polystyrene
  microplastics (0.1, 1, and 10 mg/L) for 21 days using
  mass spectrometry-based metabolomics and enzymatic biomarker
  analyses. <strong>Microplastic exposure induced oxidative stress
  and disrupted antioxidant defense systems, with significant
  reductions in SOD activity at 0.1 mg/L and CAT activity at
  0.1 and 10 mg/L.</strong> Notably, <strong>the lowest exposure
  concentration (0.1 mg/L) produced the greatest metabolic
  disturbance, with 106 differential metabolites identified,
  compared with 72 and 75 at 1 and 10 mg/L, respectively.</strong>
  Metabolomic analysis identified 867 metabolites and revealed
  significant alterations in amino acid metabolism, lipid
  metabolism, and aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis. <strong>Key amino
  acids, including aspartic acid and tyrosine, were significantly
  reduced, suggesting disturbances in energy metabolism and
  protein synthesis.</strong> Furthermore, microplastic exposure
  altered lipid-related metabolites and pathways associated with
  oxidative stress and inflammatory responses. <strong>Different
  exposure concentrations produced distinct metabolic profiles
  rather than a simple dose-dependent response.</strong> Overall,
  these findings provide mechanistic insights into microplastic-
  induced metabolic toxicity in oysters and highlight potential
  implications for seafood safety and environmental risk assessment.
