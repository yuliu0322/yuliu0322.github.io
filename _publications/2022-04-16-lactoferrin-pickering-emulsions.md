summary: >
  This study developed oleogel-based Pickering emulsions stabilized
  by transglutaminase-crosslinked lactoferrin (TG-LF) particles
  to enhance curcumin bioaccessibility. <strong>The optimal
  crosslinking conditions were pH 8, 100 U/g transglutaminase,
  50 °C, and 2 h, producing TG-LF particles with a size of
  approximately 160.9 nm.</strong> Enzymatic crosslinking reduced
  free amino groups by 65% and increased the contact angle from
  36.95° to 79.30°, improving particle wettability for Pickering
  emulsion stabilization. Three oleogel-based Pickering emulsions
  were prepared using carnauba wax (CW), behenyl alcohol (BA),
  and their mixture (MT) as gelators. <strong>CW oleogel-based
  Pickering emulsions exhibited the smallest droplet size
  (12.07 μm) and the highest storage modulus, indicating
  a stronger gel-like structure.</strong> In vitro gastrointestinal
  digestion demonstrated that <strong>CW oleogel-based Pickering
  emulsions achieved the highest lipid digestion rate and
  curcumin bioaccessibility (approximately 74%), compared
  with approximately 57% for MT, 39% for BA, and only 16%
  for pure corn oil.</strong> The enhanced bioaccessibility
  was primarily attributed to the smaller emulsion droplets,
  greater interfacial area, and increased lipid digestion,
  which facilitated mixed micelle formation and curcumin
  solubilization. Overall, <strong>TG-LF particle-stabilized
  oleogel-based Pickering emulsions, particularly those
  formulated with carnauba wax, demonstrated strong potential
  as delivery systems for improving the bioaccessibility
  of lipophilic bioactive compounds.</strong>
