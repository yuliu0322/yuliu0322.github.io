summary: >
This study investigated the effects of different cooling rates (1, 4, 8, and 16 °C/h) on the physiological metabolism, survival, and flavor quality of thick-shell mussels during low-temperature semi-anhydrous living-preservation. **A cooling rate of 4 °C/h demonstrated the best overall preservation performance, maintaining a 100% survival rate after 2 days and the highest survival rate after 7 days of storage.** Compared with other treatments, **the 4 °C/h group maintained higher ATP levels and adenosine energy charge (AEC), indicating better energy status and physiological vitality.** Flavor analysis revealed that **this cooling rate minimized the accumulation of bitter amino acids, particularly arginine, while helping preserve desirable taste characteristics.** Additionally, GC-IMS analysis demonstrated that **gradient cooling slowed the loss of volatile aroma compounds**, contributing to better flavor retention. The 4 °C/h treatment also maintained relatively higher levels of nutritionally important fatty acids, including EPA and DHA. Overall, **the findings identified 4 °C/h as a favorable cooling rate for maintaining mussel survival, physiological stability, and flavor quality**, providing practical insights for optimizing low-temperature storage and transportation of live shellfish.
