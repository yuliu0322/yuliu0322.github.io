summary: >
  This study compared the physiological and flavor responses of thick-shell
  mussels subjected to different cooling rates during low-temperature
  semi-anhydrous living-preservation. Cooling rate influenced metabolic
  activity, energy utilization, physiological stress, and the accumulation of
  flavor-related compounds during storage. Mussels cooled at 4 °C/h exhibited
  the lowest accumulation of bitter amino acids after two days, indicating
  that an appropriate cooling rate can reduce stress-induced quality
  deterioration and help preserve desirable flavor during cold-chain storage.
