category: "Seafood Safety"
summary: >
  This review comprehensively examined microplastic contamination
  in oysters, focusing on analytical methods, toxicological hazards,
  and depuration strategies. <strong>Previous studies reported
  microplastics in 94.4% of oysters globally, with an average
  abundance of 1.41 ± 0.33 particles per gram of wet tissue.</strong>
  A comparative evaluation of extraction, quantification, and
  identification techniques revealed that <strong>differences in
  sample digestion and analytical procedures can substantially
  affect microplastic quantification, highlighting the urgent
  need for standardized detection protocols.</strong> The review
  further demonstrated that <strong>microplastic exposure can
  induce oxidative stress, disrupt energy metabolism, impair
  reproductive and developmental processes, and increase
  mortality in oysters.</strong> Microplastics may also act as
  carriers for heavy metals, antibiotics, and pathogenic
  microorganisms, potentially introducing additional risks.
  Importantly, <strong>depuration was identified as a promising
  approach for reducing microplastic contamination, with
  previous studies reporting removal efficiencies of 25%
  after 3 days, 73% after 5 days, and 84.6% after 7 days.</strong>
  However, smaller microplastic particles generally required
  longer depuration periods, and extended depuration did not
  always result in further removal. <strong>Optimizing depuration
  conditions while maintaining oyster quality, together with
  standardized analytical methods and environmentally realistic
  toxicity assessments, was identified as a key priority
  for future research.</strong> Overall, this review provides
  insights into microplastic contamination, its potential
  ecological and food safety implications, and strategies
  for improving the safety of oyster consumption.
