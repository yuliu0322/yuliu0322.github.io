summary: >
  This study investigated sex-specific lipid metabolic differences
  in thick-shell mussels (Mytilus coruscus) using UPLC-HRMS-based
  lipidomics. <strong>Female mussels exhibited higher levels of
  glycerophospholipids, particularly phosphatidylcholine (PC)
  and phosphatidylethanolamine (PE), along with greater
  enrichment of polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs), whereas
  males showed higher proportions of glycerolipids and
  saturated fatty acids (SFAs).</strong> Multivariate analysis
  revealed clear separation between male and female lipidomic
  profiles. <strong>A total of 140 differential lipid species
  were identified, including 80 triacylglycerols (TGs),
  26 PCs, 13 diacylglycerols (DGs), and 10 PEs, highlighting
  extensive sex-dependent lipid remodeling.</strong> Notably,
  <strong>female mussels exhibited greater enrichment of
  structural phospholipids and ceramide species, suggesting
  enhanced membrane remodeling and sphingolipid-associated
  regulation, while males displayed lipid profiles more
  closely associated with neutral lipid storage.</strong>
  KEGG pathway analysis identified glycerophospholipid
  metabolism as the most significantly enriched pathway,
  alongside glycerolipid metabolism and PUFA-related pathways.
  <strong>These findings suggest distinct lipid metabolic
  strategies between sexes, potentially reflecting differences
  in reproductive demands, membrane homeostasis, and energy
  allocation.</strong> Overall, this study provides molecular-level
  insights into sex-dependent lipid metabolism and supports
  improved nutritional evaluation and targeted utilization
  of mussel resources.
